CHAPTER ONE

Where did Halloween begin?

Halloween is connected to the ancient Celtic festival of Samhain, which marked the seasonal transition into winter. Over centuries, local customs, Christian traditions, and popular culture shaped the modern celebration.

Samhain: an ancient Celtic festival in Ireland and Scotland marking the end of the harvest around October 31–November 1. Folklore held that the boundary between the living and the dead grew thin on this night.

All Hallows’ Eve: All Saints’ Day falls on November 1 in the Christian calendar; its eve became known as All Hallows’ Eve, later shortened to Halloween.

Jack-o’-lanterns: in Ireland, early lanterns were carved from turnips. Immigrants brought the tradition to North America, where pumpkins became popular. Modern trick-or-treating spread in the United States and Canada during the 20th century.

Halloween’s history blends many traditions rather than stemming from a single, simple origin.
